**Agent Name:** Wortgraph Coach

## What it does

Wortgraph turns the German you actually read into a personal knowledge graph in
Neo4j Aura — then coaches you over it with a published Aura Agent.

Every vocabulary app stores words as a flat list. But the human mental lexicon
is a network: you recall a word through its connections. That mismatch is why
flashcards leak. Wortgraph stores vocabulary the way your brain does — as a
graph — and the agent answers questions a list-based app structurally cannot:

- *"What new word should I add next?"* → the **bridge word** that connects
  clusters you already know
- *"Which weak word do I review first, and why?"* → ranked by
  **connectivity × forgetting**, not just by score
- *"What am I worst at?"* → a collapsing **topic cluster**, not one stray word

## The learning loop that feeds the graph

📸 4 — Paste real German (a news piece on Guantánamo); Groq extracts the words
worth learning — each verified against the Wiktionary API + DWDS corpus, so a
hallucinated word can never enter the graph.
📸 5 — Study list: CEFR levels, retention, review counts per source.
📸 6 — Flashcards treat grammar as part of the word: der/die/das drilled with
every noun, real example sentences; grading writes retention into the graph.
📸 7 — Games are generated by Cypher: Odd-One-Out pulls 3 grouped words + 1
outlier from the graph, explains WHY, and shows the query that built the round.
📸 8 — The same words become a crossword — this morning's article is tonight's
puzzle.

## Dataset and why a graph fits

**Dataset:** the learner's own reading, continuously extracted, verified,
embedded (gemini-embedding-001, 3,072-dim vector index) and enriched by a
Graph-RAG pipeline: vector retrieval grounds an LLM that classifies
synonym/antonym/word-form relations, written back as typed edges. Demo deck:
139 words, ~700 co-occurrence edges, 8 topics, 3,500+ relationships graph-wide.

📸 9 — The deck as a graph: every saved word linked by CO_OCCURS_WITH.
📸 10 — Meaning hubs: one English concept, every German word that expresses it.

**Why a graph fits — the bridge-word insight.** The flagship feature *is* a
traversal: a word you don't know, co-occurring with ≥2 words you do know:

```cypher
MATCH (u:User {id:'demo'})-[:ADDED]->(known:Word)
MATCH (known)-[:CO_OCCURS_WITH]-(candidate:Word)
WHERE NOT EXISTS { (u)-[:ADDED]->(candidate) }
WITH candidate, count(DISTINCT known) AS bridgeDegree,
     collect(DISTINCT known.lemma)[0..5] AS connectedTo
WHERE bridgeDegree >= 2
RETURN candidate.lemma, bridgeDegree, connectedTo
ORDER BY bridgeDegree DESC LIMIT 5
```

One `MATCH` in Cypher; recursive self-join misery in SQL. Same for graph-aware
spaced repetition: `degree × (1 − retention)` — a connected word you barely
know outranks an isolated one.

**Schema:**
```
(:User)-[:ADDED {retention, reviewCount}]->(:Word {lemma, article, cefr, translation, embedding[3072]})
(:Word)-[:CO_OCCURS_WITH {strength}]-(:Word)
(:Word)-[:BELONGS_TO]->(:Topic)
(:Word)-[:MEANS]->(:Meaning)
(:Word)-[:SYNONYM_OF|ANTONYM_OF|FORM_OF {confidence, reason}]->(:Word)
```

**Why this needed Neo4j specifically:**

1. **Vector search and graph traversal in one query.** Graph-RAG calls
   `db.index.vector.queryNodes('word_embeddings', …)` and walks the learner's
   `ADDED` edges in the same Cypher statement — one round trip, one source of
   truth, where the conventional stack needs a vector store + a relational DB +
   an application-side join.
2. **The game logic *is* the query language.** "Three words sharing a meaning
   hub plus one outlier" is a single Cypher pattern — the graph pattern is the
   game rule, which is why games can't hallucinate.
3. **The database is the interface.** The Graph and Clusters views render the
   stored structure itself as the learning surface — in a graph, the shape
   carries the meaning.

## Why this matters: graphs for education

Among agents for healthcare, finance, and cybersecurity, Wortgraph brings the
knowledge graph to **education** — one of the most personal datasets there is:
*what a learner knows*. For the immigrants and refugees among the world's
German learners, vocabulary retention isn't a hobby metric — it's the speed at
which a new life starts working.

**Where this goes with more attention:**
- **Any language, same graph** — swap the corpus verification (Wiktionary
  covers 180+ languages) and the bridge-word engine works for Spanish, Arabic,
  or Ukrainian.
- **Per-learner published agents** — every user gets an agent scoped to their
  own subgraph.
- **Classroom graphs** — a teacher's view over the merged class graph: which
  bridge word unlocks the most for the most students? Same Cypher, one MATCH
  wider.
- **MCP everywhere** — connected to the assistants learners already use, every
  AI conversation becomes vocabulary practice calibrated to their level.

index) · Wiktionary + DWDS verification · Express · React.

Flashcard apps store your effort as a list and let it leak. Wortgraph stores it
as a graph — where every word you add strengthens the ones you already have.
